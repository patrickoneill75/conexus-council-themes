Skills to build

  • Ranking: the original STARs model, unchanged. Occupations are ordered by Euclidean distance across 35 O*NET skill-importance scores. A smaller distance means a closer skill profile.
  • Skill match: the same distance as a 0 to 100% figure, scaled against the largest distance two occupations could have. It always agrees with the ranking.
  • Wage gain: the percentage increase from that occupation's Indiana annual median wage to the wage of the job you need to fill.
  • Wage matching: Indiana wages use the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) May 2019/2020 hybrid Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics (OEWS) structure, matched by title and Standard Occupational Classification (SOC) code first, then the official BLS hybrid crosswalk where needed.
  • Wage filter: when checked, results only include occupations with a lower Indiana annual median wage than the job you need to fill.
  • Skills to build: skills more important in the job you need to fill than in the other occupation.
  • Shortfall: the other occupation's shortfall against the job's O*NET importance score: (target − source) / target.
  • Important: this screens occupation-level talent pools. It does not assess an individual applicant and does not replace required licenses, credentials, safety training or employer-specific qualifications.

Skills to build

  • Start with your current occupation: the model treats your current job as evidence of skills you may already have built through work experience.
  • Ranking: other occupations are ranked by the same Euclidean distance across 35 O*NET skill-importance scores that the employer tool uses.
  • Skill match: the same distance as a 0 to 100% figure. A higher figure means a skill profile closer to your current job, and it always agrees with the ranking.
  • Wage gain: the percentage increase from your current occupation's Indiana annual median wage to the other occupation's.
  • Wage filter: when checked, results only include occupations with a higher Indiana annual median wage than your current occupation.
  • Skills to build: picking an occupation compares it with your current job and lists the skills more important in the new job.
  • Shortfall: your current occupation's shortfall against the new job's O*NET importance score: (target − current) / target.
  • Important: this explores careers at the occupation level. It does not assess a person's actual skills, qualifications, education, licenses or employer-specific requirements.
